# Dira Creative — UX, Decision Design & First-Visit Onboarding

## Product promise

Dira Creative, the operating brand of Royalty Holdings (Pty) Ltd, helps startups, growing businesses and established organisations build brands that communicate clearly, operate efficiently and scale effectively.

The experience should make the next sensible step obvious. Visitors should not need to understand agency terminology before they can begin.

## Audience and decision paths

- **Starting out:** needs a credible foundation and a clear first brand system. Primary fit: Novice Brand Package (P3,200).
- **Growing:** needs consistency across brand identity, presence and day-to-day business. Primary fit: Elevate Brand Package (P5,200).
- **Established:** needs a complete connected identity and broader strategic application. Primary fit: Elite Brand Package (P12,500).
- **Not sure yet:** should be able to ask for a discovery conversation rather than guess at a technical service.

These are useful starting points, not eligibility rules. A customer may choose any individual service or request a custom scope.

## Service architecture

Keep the five development stages visible as the agency's organising model:

1. Define Your Identity — logo, colour, typography, iconography, graphic elements, photography direction, illustration and layout.
2. Establish Your Presence — profiles, proposals, portfolios, pitch decks, business cards, websites, applications and maintenance.
3. Structure Your Operations — workflows, team structures, function mapping and operational support.
4. Connect With Your Market — flyers, brochures, posters, advertising, social media, video ads and motion graphics.
5. Optimise for Growth — assets and resources, revenue optimisation, gap analysis, strategic problem-solving and growth solutions.

Show the most useful starting choices first, while keeping the complete catalogue searchable and available. Preserve the supplied BWP prices and show one published package price using the former range maximum; scope and any price adjustment are discussed and agreed before approval. Never turn a custom price into zero or imply an estimate is a final quote.

## Psychology-informed interaction decisions

- **Reduce choice overload without removing choice.** Lead with three audience-level package paths and a small recommended service set. Keep every other service reachable through search and category filters. Do not create a flat wall of equally weighted options.
- **Recognition before recall.** Use plain-language audience labels ("I'm getting started", "I'm ready to grow", "I'm established") alongside agency service names.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Ask for the essential information first. Keep budget, timing and working preference optional and collapsed until requested. Never hide required scope or contact details.
- **Build one project basket.** Customers can add or remove multiple catalogue items in one project enquiry. Retain each item’s published price and review combined scope before issuing a final quote.
- **Set expectations early.** Explain that an enquiry is reviewed, scoped and quoted before work or payment is confirmed. A preferred date is not a confirmed delivery date.
- **Use a short, visible process.** Share brief → scope/proposal → approval/begin so customers understand what happens after they submit.
- **Keep the user in control.** The service guide is optional, can be dismissed, and never blocks browsing or form completion.
- **Make actions and outcomes legible.** Buttons name the outcome; loading, errors and confirmations say what really happened. Do not display public phone numbers, email addresses or contact panels in the storefront or customer account. Collect customer contact details only inside the private order/account journey. The order fallback must clearly say that a WhatsApp draft is prepared and is not sent until the customer presses Send.
- **Avoid manipulative choice design.** Do not preselect expensive packages, hide lower-priced options, invent scarcity, fabricate testimonials or use false urgency.

## Brand and visual rules

- Core brand colours: Dark Grey #222222, Dark Turquoise #0097b2, Azure #4bb4ce, Light Blue #f4f6fc.
- Keep each experience visually restrained. For customer-selected theme systems, allow no more than three active accent colours; neutral text and surface colours remain protected and must not be replaced by low-contrast accent combinations.
- Derive foreground colours from actual contrast, not from assumptions that every accent supports white text.
- Use Radnika Next for headings/primary typography and Open Sans for supporting text where the assets are available; use readable system fallbacks otherwise.
- Body and control text >=14px, supporting metadata >=12px, comfortable line-height, WCAG AA contrast.
- Follow the Iteration 13 visual direction and inspect the real reference image for visual QA. Apply the quality, hierarchy, compactness and clear action priority—not the reference business identity or palette.

## First QA acceptance journeys

1. Visitor recognises Dira Creative and its purpose in the first viewport.
2. Visitor can choose a startup/growth/established package or browse the full catalogue.
3. Visitor can search/filter services and read complete descriptions and prices without hover.
4. Choosing a service carries the service and package into the enquiry form.
5. A visitor starting without a selection can still choose/type a service or ask for discovery help.
6. The form starts with only essential fields; optional details remain optional.
7. If online submission is unavailable, the form offers a transparent WhatsApp draft and never claims the enquiry was saved.
8. Client account and owner workspace share a consistent brand, hierarchy and compact component system.
9. Owner can distinguish new enquiries, quotes, approved work and verified payments without confusing projections with money received.
10. Test narrow mobile, wider mobile/tablet and desktop layouts; no clipped essential text or hidden actions.
